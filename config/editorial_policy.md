# Editorial Policy — Lehigh Valley Morning Briefing

## Sourcing

- Every factual claim must be traceable to a named source (outlet, agency, or
  official record).
- Prefer primary sources: government press releases, official meeting minutes,
  NWS data, PennDOT feeds. Use news outlets for reporting that adds context
  beyond raw primary data.
- Minimum of one source per story. Two independent sources preferred for any
  claim that is contested, surprising, or consequential.
- Never present AI-generated summaries as original reporting. The briefing
  synthesizes and cites; it does not investigate.

## Freshness

- Only include information published or updated within the past 24 hours.
- For ongoing stories, reference the latest development — do not rehash
  background unless it is necessary for a new listener to understand the update.
- Weather and traffic data must be from the current day's forecast or real-time
  feeds.

## No Filler

- Every sentence must deliver information the listener needs or can act on.
- No greetings beyond a short show open (e.g., date and "Here's what's
  happening in the Lehigh Valley today.").
- No sign-off banter, self-promotion, or padding to fill time.
- If a section has nothing substantive, omit it entirely rather than filling
  with low-value material.

## No Opinion

- Report facts. Do not characterize events as good, bad, exciting,
  controversial, or any other evaluative framing.
- Do not speculate on motives, outcomes, or implications beyond what sources
  state on the record.
- Attribute characterizations to their source when quoting (e.g., "The mayor
  called the proposal 'a step forward'" — not "The proposal is a step
  forward").

## No Unverified Claims

- Do not include information from anonymous social-media posts, unverified
  tips, or single unnamed sources.
- If a story is developing and key facts are unconfirmed, either wait for
  confirmation or clearly state what is and is not confirmed.
- Do not repeat rumors, even with a disclaimer. If it is not confirmed, leave
  it out.

## Local Relevance

- Every item must have a direct connection to the Lehigh Valley (Lehigh County,
  Northampton County, or immediately adjacent communities).
- National or state-level stories are included only when there is a specific,
  concrete local impact (e.g., a federal highway funding decision affecting a
  local project).
- "Of interest to locals" is not enough — there must be a tangible local angle.
